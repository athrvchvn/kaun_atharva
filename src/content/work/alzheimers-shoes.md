## The idea

Changes in how a person walks — stride, rhythm, how weight moves across the foot — can show up
years before other signs of Alzheimer's. A shoe is worn every day, so it could watch for those
changes continuously, at home, without anyone having to remember to do a test.

The plan was a shoe with **TENG sensors** in the sole: self-powered, like the army boots I had
worked on in the same lab, reading the pressure pattern of each step and passing it to a model
trained to spot abnormal gait.

## Why I stopped

I stopped, for three reasons:

1. **My own expertise ceiling** at the time.
2. **TENG wasn't showing promise** as the sensor for this.
3. **The training data** would have needed hospital access to patients.

It's here because stopping was the right call, and a page of only finished things would be
misleading.
